[Reading] The Many Lives & Secret Sorrows of Josephine B., Sandra Gulland

Armand Pizzicarola · Aug 6, 2021

This book was recommended to me by one of our investors, and also my first conscious foray into historical fiction. Apparently, this genre is much more popular than I previously realized, and as a result of curiously crosschecking facts, which makes me feel like an investigator, it has ended up being one of my favorite genres to read.

In short, this is a depiction of events from the perspective of Josephine Bonaparte, starting as a young girl. Napoleon is not the focal point of this book; he only arrives in the later half.

  • Perspective matters. In a similar vein to the saying “History is Always Written by The Victors”, it’s rare that we get a chance to view major historical events from a side lens.
  • The French Revolution was complicated. I’m not a history buff, so maybe it’s just me here, but I left the book having more questions about the period of time than I did going into it. The book is by no means meant to be an account of the French Revolution, but it obviously is the driving force behind many outcomes.
  • The last location I expected this book to be set in was on a beach. I’m oversimplifying it here, but only as the first few chapters moved along was I able to fully connect the dots.
